# Long-side intraday setups on low-priced, low-float news runners: what the evidence actually says

Method note (read first): web page fetching was blocked in this session (every direct page/PDF fetch failed at DNS), so these notes rest on search-engine extracts of abstracts, journal listings and secondary write-ups, not on the full texts. Numbers quoted below are as reported in those extracts; where two sources disagree it is noted. Evidence grades used throughout: **[PEER]** peer-reviewed journal; **[WP]** working paper / preprint; **[BT-affil]** large backtest by authors with a commercial interest; **[BT-indep]** independent replication/backtest; **[REG]** regulator / exchange study; **[VENDOR]** vendor, course or blog claim with undisclosed method; **[ANEC]** anecdote.

## 1. Gap-and-go: buying the premarket-high break or the open on day-one news gappers

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study or large cost-inclusive public backtest of "buy the premarket-high break on a day-one low-float news gapper" was found. The closest rigorous evidence points the other way for the open itself: stocks that grab retail attention overnight tend to open too high and give back during the day, and the hidden cost of buying them at the open exceeds the quoted spread. News-backed moves do tend to keep drifting over days to weeks, which is the main argument for the long side - but that drift is measured close-to-close, not from a chased intraday entry.

### Cited Findings
**Attention, the open, and intraday reversal**
- [PEER] Berkman, Koch, Tuttle & Zhang, "Paying Attention: Overnight Returns and the Hidden Cost of Buying at the Open," JFQA 47(4), 2012: high-attention stocks show positive overnight returns that reverse during the trading day, driven by an opening price that is high relative to intraday prices; the effect is concentrated in stocks that recently drew retail attention, is stronger in hard-to-value and costly-to-arbitrage stocks, and is larger when retail sentiment is high. The extra implicit cost for retail traders who buy high-attention stocks near the open "frequently exceed[s] the effective half spread." Mechanism: an attention event on day t produces retail buying at the open of day t+1, the first chance to buy. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v47y2012i04p715-741_00.html); [KU working-paper PDF](https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu/bitstream/1808/10965/1/Koch_Paying%20Attention.pdf)
- [WP] A 2025 arXiv paper, "Does Overnight News Explain Overnight Returns?", summarizes Berkman et al. as: retail investors bid up the price at the open for the previous day's high-attention stocks, so a high overnight return is followed by a low intraday return; it notes Berkman et al. measured attention only indirectly (squared returns, net buying). — [arXiv 2507.04481](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04481)
- [PEER] Aboody, Even-Tov, Lehavy & Trueman, "Overnight Returns and Firm-Specific Investor Sentiment," JFQA 53(2), 2018: overnight returns persist over the short run (several weeks), more so for hard-to-value firms and firms with more individual / fewer institutional holders, then reverse over the longer term (stocks with high overnight returns underperform over the next ~12 months). Sample start July 1992; end date reported as Dec 2013 by Berkeley's newsroom and Dec 2015 by U. Michigan's release (conflict). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v53y2018i02p485-505_00.html); [UMich release](https://news.umich.edu/?p=24673); [Berkeley Haas release](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/news-release/how-after-hours-trading-sheds-light-on-investor-sentiment/)
- [PEER/WP] Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, "Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users" (published J. Finance 2022; figures here from working drafts): the stocks Robinhood users herd into each day earn average 5-day abnormal returns of about -3% (about -6% for the most extreme herding); press coverage cites about -5% over the next month and -9% for the most extreme events. The negative returns are not explained by the bid-ask spread, are stronger after March 2020, and the authors tie the herding to the app's "Top Movers" list steering attention to the same few stocks. — [SSRN draft PDF mirror](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/SSRN-id3715077.pdf); [UC Davis summary](https://gsm.ucdavis.edu/news/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market)

**News vs. no-news: does a gap on real news keep going?**
- [PEER] Chan, "Stock price reaction to news and no-news: drift and reversal after headlines," JFE 70(2), 2003: stocks with public headline news drift in the news direction afterwards (strongest after bad news); stocks with similar-sized moves but no identifiable news show some reversal, concentrated in smaller stocks. Horizon is monthly, not intraday. — [SUFE newsletter abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357702)
- [PEER] Savor, "Stock returns after major price shocks: The impact of information," JFE 106(3), 2012: large price moves accompanied by information (analyst reports) are followed by drift; moves without information reverse. Drift exists only when the price move and the analyst-recommendation change point the same way. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p635-659.html); [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/fnce_papers/387)
- [PEER] Jiang & Zhu, "Information Shocks and Short-Term Market Underreaction," JFE 124(1), 2017: using large price jumps as a proxy for news, stocks that jumped up keep outperforming over the next one to three months (underreaction); intraday and especially overnight jumps show the same pattern; attributed to limited attention. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v124y2017i1p43-64.html)
- [WP] Kargarzadeh, Khaledian, Parvini, Ghatak & Khaledian, "Buy the Rumor, Sell the News: When Is News Priced In?" (arXiv 2608.14014, Aug 2026): 4.57 million news articles on ~3,000 US stocks, 2023-2026, ~1.68 million stock-day events with a placebo of 364,405 neutral events. Most of the news-related move happens by the close of the publication day (pooled, the day-0 move in the news direction is ~2.8x its size at day +20, i.e. much is given back); for rumors, the rumor day captures the whole move and confirmation adds nothing; launch/partnership news (96-97% positive in tone) appears to overshoot, and a "fade small-cap launch and partnership news" rule earned 15.8% annualized **gross** (before costs), Sharpe 1.35. Caveats from the authors and a reviewer: descriptive not causal; a tag-matched robustness check pushes the launch estimate across zero; magnitudes are model-dependent. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14014); [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2608.14014)

**Premarket prices**
- [PEER] Barclay & Hendershott, "Price discovery and trading after hours," RFS 16(4), 2003: low after-hours volume produces significant but inefficient price discovery; individual after-hours trades carry more information than daytime trades; pre-open price changes are less noisy than post-close changes. (Author listing is inconsistent across indexes.) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i4p1041-1073.html)
- [PEER] Barclay & Hendershott, J. Empirical Finance 15(5), 2008: as Nasdaq pre-open volume grew, the opening price became more efficient and price discovery moved into the pre-open, but a critical threshold of volume is needed before pre-open trading adds information to the opening price. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v15y2008i5p839-849.html)

**Gap statistics (mostly vendor or large-cap)**
- [VENDOR] SmallCapLab dashboard: running average 65.6% of small-cap gap-ups close below their regular-session open; for stocks gapping 40%+ that open 50%+ below their own premarket high, 57 of 81 (~70%) closed below the open. Universe, period and float filters not documented. — [SmallCapLab research](https://www.smallcaplab.com/research)
- [ANEC] Trade The Matrix blog: a small cap that fades and closes under the open "had a 75% chance" (2019 data, no method). — [tradethematrix.net](https://www.tradethematrix.net/post/behavior-of-small-cap-stocks)
- [BT-indep, large caps] Kai Yasuda's SPY/QQQ gap-fade test: gap-fill rates ran ~95% on the smallest gaps to under 20% on the biggest, yet fading QQQ gaps >2% lost money (profit factor 0.65, 39.6% of 275 trades profitable) because stops were hit before fills. Shows a high "fill rate" or "fade rate" does not equal a profitable trade once stops are in. — [Substack](https://kaiyasuda.substack.com/p/the-gap-fill-teardown)
- [PEER/WP, large caps] DJIA stocks: small open gaps usually close the same session, large gaps are hard to close intraday; weekend gaps in DJIA/Nasdaq tend to continue for several hours after Monday's open; in index futures, the larger the opening return the larger the rebound. — [DJIA gap study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336073716_Filling_Open_Price_Gap_on_Intraday_Timeframe_A_Case_Study_for_DJIA_Index_Stocks); [MDPI weekend gaps](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/18/3/132); [index futures reversals](https://scispace.com/pdf/intraday-price-reversals-for-index-futures-in-the-us-and-137ieyy6y7.pdf)
- [WP] A 2026 pre-registered "falsification" study on Micro Nasdaq futures reports that tested gap-continuation and gap-fade rules fail out of sample after transaction costs (futures, not small caps; attribution from search extract). — [arXiv 2605.04004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04004)

**Costs on low-priced stocks**
- Arithmetic: a $0.05 spread is 100 bps on a $5 stock vs 1 bp on a $500 stock. — [Tapeboard glossary](https://tapeboard.com/glossary/what-is-the-bid-ask-spread)
- [VENDOR] Rule-of-thumb bands: small caps ($500M-$2B) 0.25-1.5% spreads; penny stocks 5-25%+ (unsourced). — [StockAlarm](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/bid-ask-spread-explained)
- [WP, non-US] Small stocks' spreads are almost 10x large stocks' on Nasdaq Nordic; ~1% relative spread is common for small stocks. — [Swedish House of Finance brief](https://www.hhs.se/globalassets/swedish-house-of-finance/data-center/research-brief---the-retail-investment-boom-and-the-cost-of-trading-small-stocks-1-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a day-one news gapper, the academic evidence supports "the news is real, the stock will tend to be higher in weeks" (Chan, Savor, Jiang & Zhu) and, separately, "the open on a retail-attention stock is a bad price" (Berkman et al., Barber et al.). These are compatible: the long edge, if any, is in catalyst quality, not in paying the open/premarket high.
- Chan and Savor together give a usable filter: a big move with no identifiable news is a reversal candidate, a big move with real news is a drift candidate. A bot that buys spikes without a confirmed catalyst is on the wrong side of the documented pattern.
- Kargarzadeh et al. (2026) suggests "partnership/launch" press releases - the typical low-float runner catalyst - are the category most prone to overshoot; earnings/fundamental news is the category more likely to drift. Gross-only and fragile, so treat as a hypothesis to check against the bot's own logs, not a rule.
- Thin premarket trading is real but noisy price discovery (Barclay & Hendershott); a premarket high printed on little volume is a weaker reference level than one printed on heavy volume.
- Vendor gap statistics (65-75% of small-cap gap-ups close below the open) are consistent with the academic attention-reversal result but cannot be relied on for sizing an edge; and the large-cap gap-fade test shows how a high base rate can still lose money once stops are applied.
- On a $2-$5 stock, a 1-2 cent spread plus a cent or two of slippage each way is roughly 0.5%-2% round trip before any adverse-selection ("hidden") cost - large relative to the per-trade edges documented anywhere in this file.

### Gaps
- No published, cost-inclusive test of buying the premarket-high break on day-one low-float gappers was found; vendor claims in this space do not disclose universe, survivorship handling or fills.
- Berkman et al.'s exact magnitudes (size of the open-to-close reversal, its size for the top attention decile) could not be read; only the abstract-level direction is confirmed.
- Whether the attention-reversal effect holds for $1-$20 low-float stocks specifically (vs. the broad CRSP universe in these papers) is untested in the sources found.
- No source quantified realized slippage for market/stop orders on sub-$5 low-float names; a US study of effective spreads by price bucket was not located.

## 2. Opening range breakout (ORB) on "Stocks in Play" (Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz 2024) - findings, universe, costs, transfer to sub-$20 low-float names

### Takeaway
The Stocks-in-Play ORB paper is the strongest public intraday-stock backtest, but it is not peer-reviewed, one co-author sells day-trader education, it trades both long and short, its universe excludes stocks under $5, and its cost model (commissions; slippage not evident) is light for small, fast names. Its most robust message is that plain ORB barely works and that almost all the edge comes from trading only the day's most unusually active stocks (highest relative volume). Average edge per trade is small (about +0.08R for relative volume above 1x, higher only in the extreme relative-volume bucket), so realistic slippage on low-float names could erase much of it.

### Cited Findings
- [BT-affil / WP] Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz, "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy For The U.S. Equity Market," SSRN 4729284, Feb 2024: 5-minute ORB tested on 7,000+ US stocks, 2016-2023; also compared 15-, 30-, 60-minute ranges (5-minute best). "Stocks in Play" = stocks trading far above normal volume, mostly due to company news. Top-20 Stocks-in-Play portfolio: >1,600% total net return (1,637% in another summary), 41.6% annualized, Sharpe 2.81, annualized alpha 36%, beta near zero, vs ~198% for the S&P 500 over the same period. Authors say the benefit of restricting to Stocks in Play holds "even after considering transaction costs." — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4729284); [Concretum Group page](https://concretumgroup.com/a-profitable-day-trading-strategy-for-the-u-s-equity-market/); [LetsDataScience summary](https://www.letsdatascience.com/news/traders-adopt-orb-strategy-for-stocks-in-play-19394da2)
- Rules as summarized by QuantifiedStrategies (secondary): eligibility price > $5, 14-day average volume > 1,000,000 shares, 14-day ATR > $0.50; "in play" = relative volume in the first 5 minutes at least 100%, then trade only the top 20 by relative volume; if the first 5-minute candle closes up, take only a long on a break of its high; if down, only a short below its low; ATR-based stop; flat by the close. — [QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/stocks-in-play-trading-strategy-day-trading/); [QuantifiedStrategies Substack](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/stocks-in-play-opening-range-breakout)
- Edge by relative volume (secondary summary of the paper): relative volume below 100% averaged -0.02R per trade; above 100% averaged +0.08R; above 30x averaged +0.38R. Unfiltered ORB across liquid stocks: about 29% total over the period (danfin: 3.2% annualized, Sharpe 0.48), worse than buy-and-hold. — [QuantifiedStrategies Substack](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/stocks-in-play-opening-range-breakout); [danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)
- [BT-indep] QuantConnect re-implementation (top 1,000 most liquid US stocks, price > $5, ATR > $0.50): Sharpe ~2.4, beta near zero; parameters not fully verified. — [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)
- Critique (danfin.net): "selecting the day's most unusually active stocks by opening relative volume did almost all the work"; headline returns are backtests with simplified execution. — [danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)
- Conflict of interest: co-author Andrew Aziz runs the Bear Bull Traders day-trading community, which promotes the paper. — [Wikipedia: Andrew Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Aziz); [Bear Bull Traders newsletter](https://bearbulltraders.com/category/andrews-newsletter/page/3/)
- Earlier sibling paper, Zarattini & Aziz, "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" (SSRN 4416622, 2023): 5-minute ORB on QQQ/TQQQ; the model assumes no slippage and its 9,350% variant came from a parameter search. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4416622); [danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)
- [BT-indep] A practitioner replication of the QQQ ORB rule on five index CFDs: gross results reproduced, net of costs indistinguishable from zero ("gross reproduced, net zero"). — [MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- [WP] Fetna, "Opening-Range Breakout Does Not Survive Trading Costs: A Pre-Registered 225-Cell Study on Sixteen Years of Futures Data" (SSRN 7428398) - title-level only; futures. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7428398)

### Inferences
- Transfer to $1-$20 low-float runners is partial at best: the paper's universe floor is $5 with >1M average daily volume and >$0.50 ATR, so most $1-$5 low-float names, and many recent IPO/low-history names, are outside it. Its stocks-in-play are mostly fundamental-news names among liquid stocks, not micro-float squeezes.
- The paper's portfolio is long and short together with near-zero beta; the long half alone was not separately reported in the sources found. A long-only bot cannot assume the headline Sharpe.
- What transfers well is the ranking idea: the edge rises steeply with relative volume (−0.02R → +0.08R → +0.38R). That fits the owner's "#1 and #2 leaders first" rule - trade only the most unusually active names, not everything that gaps.
- Cost sensitivity: at +0.08R average, round-trip slippage of ~8% of the stop distance wipes out the edge. With a tight stop on a fast low-float stock (e.g. a 10-cent stop on a $4 stock), two cents of adverse fill each way is already 40% of R. Only the extreme relative-volume bucket (+0.38R) leaves real room for costs.
- The pattern across independent replications is the classic one: gross results reproduce, net results shrink toward zero when costs and slippage are added realistically, especially on index products.

### Gaps
- The paper's exact commission/slippage assumptions, stop multiple, leverage cap and position sizing could not be confirmed from accessible sources (they are believed to be a per-share commission with ATR-fraction stops and a leverage cap, but this could not be verified and should be checked in the PDF).
- No long-only vs. short-only breakdown, win rate, or average win/loss was found in accessible summaries.
- Whether delisted stocks were included (survivorship) is not stated in accessible summaries; the 2016-2023 period's out-of-sample performance (2024-2026) has not been published independently.
- No test of ORB on sub-$5 or low-float (<20M shares) stocks was found.

## 3. Entry timing: first pullback / VWAP reclaim / bull flag / "green-red-green" vs buying the initial burst

### Takeaway
There is no peer-reviewed or large cost-inclusive evidence that a first-pullback, VWAP-reclaim or bull-flag entry beats buying the initial burst (or vice versa) in low-float runners. The available numbers are daily-chart pattern statistics or vendor algorithm win rates with undisclosed methods. The only rigorous, related facts are microstructure ones: very short-horizon intraday returns in individual stocks tend to reverse, and liquidity-taking (aggressive) orders are where most retail losses - and the few profitable heavy day traders' gains - come from.

### Cited Findings
- [Book-based, daily charts, via secondary source] Bulkowski's pattern statistics as summarized: bull flags failed 55% of the time across 1,028 trades; "high tight flags" had an 85% success rate on upside breakouts; volume trended down during the flag in 74% of flags that broke upward; tight flags outperformed loose ones. Daily charts, not intraday, not small-cap specific. — [Liberated Stock Trader](https://liberatedstocktrader.com/?p=24413)
- [PEER, index] An early academic template-matching study found a bull-flag template on the NYSE Composite Index with a 5-day horizon gave results better than random; index-level, no win rate reported in extract. — [UCF STARS record](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/scopus2000/643)
- [VENDOR] Trade-Ideas' "Holly" AI listed a "Bullish Pullback" strategy at a 79.3% win rate in one 2018 post and 71.9% "test win %" in another two days earlier; optimized in-sample figures, method unpublished, unstable between reports. — [Trade-Ideas 2018-07-26](https://www.trade-ideas.com/2018/07/26/bullish-pullback-leads-20-algos-16-long-holly-morning-huddle/); [Trade-Ideas 2018-07-24](https://www.trade-ideas.com/2018/07/24/bullish-pullback-leads-19-algos-15-long-holly-morning-huddle/)
- [PEER] Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka, "Intraday Patterns in the Cross-Section of Stock Returns," J. Finance 65, 2010: a stock's return over a half-hour interval is negatively related to its returns over the recent intervals (bid-ask bounce and lack of resiliency), while it is positively related at lags that are exact multiples of a trading day (lasting at least 40 days), strongest in the first and last half-hours; not driven by firm size. — [ar5iv / arXiv 1005.3535](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1005.3535); [Kellogg abstract](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2010/intraday-patterns-in-the-cross-section-of-stock-returns)
- [PEER/WP] Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (Taiwan): for heavy day traders, 74% of trades come from aggressive orders and 83% of their gross profits trace to aggressive limit orders; for individuals overall, virtually all trading losses trace to aggressive orders, while passive individual trades are profitable at short horizons. — [Do Individual Day Traders Make Money? (2004 WP)](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trade%20040330.pdf); [Who Gains from Trade?](http://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/taiwan%20performance/who%20gains%20from%20trade%20040407.pdf)

### Inferences
- Short-horizon reversal at half-hour intervals (Heston et al.) means a stock that has just spiked is, on average, more likely to give some back in the next interval than to continue - a weak, indirect argument for not paying the top of the first burst and for waiting for a pullback that holds. It is indirect: those are average stocks, not news runners.
- Because the edge-per-trade in any documented intraday strategy is small, the bigger practical difference between "first spike" and "first pullback" entries is probably cost and stop distance: a pullback entry with a nearby, well-defined stop (pullback low / VWAP) gives a smaller R and lower slippage share than buying a vertical candle with a stop far below. This is reasoning, not tested evidence.
- The Taiwan result that profitable heavy day traders made most gross profit with aggressive orders means "take liquidity when the signal fires" is not inherently wrong - the losers also used aggressive orders; selection, not order type, separated them.
- The bot's own recorded bars and live fills are likely the only realistic way to settle spike-vs-pullback for this universe; per the project's standing rules, live results outrank replays.

### Gaps
- No study found that compares entry at the first breakout vs. after the first pullback / VWAP reclaim for news-driven small caps, with or without costs.
- No independent statistics on "green-red-green", micro pullback, or VWAP-reclaim patterns were found.
- Bulkowski's flag statistics were seen only through a secondary site; original sample and period not verified.

## 4. Halt-resumption (LULD) trades, momentum ignition, and buying parabolic speed bursts

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed evidence shows that buying LULD halt resumptions, or buying parabolic speed bursts, has a positive expected value. Regulator data say most LULD pauses happen in small, low-volume stocks and many reflect temporary liquidity gaps that revert; the best US academic study finds pauses add volatility and reduce liquidity after resumption. The broader literature on extreme returns (MAX/lottery stocks, low-float bubbles, no-news shocks) points to overpricing followed by reversal, not continuation. Momentum ignition is a regulator term for a potentially manipulative tactic, not a documented retail edge.

### Cited Findings
- [REG] SEC DERA white paper on the LULD pilot: after a trading pause, prices typically returned to the price bands that applied before the pause; some pauses are triggered by temporary liquidity gaps - a large price change from depleted liquidity that reverts quickly once liquidity returns. — [SEC DERA LULD white paper](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-luld-white-paper.pdf)
- [REG] LULD Supplemental Joint Assessment (exchanges): most limit states and pauses occurred in small, low-volume stocks, many unnecessary due to opening-reference-price problems; over half of all pauses occurred in just 50 of 5,000+ symbols. — [LULD Supplemental Joint Assessment](https://cdn.luldplan.com/reports/LULD-Supplemental-Joint-Assessment.pdf)
- [REG] SEC DERA on Amendment 10: pause frequency fell more than 75% after the amendment, most for Tier 2 (smaller) securities - pause counts depend heavily on rule mechanics. — [SEC DERA Amendment 10 paper](https://www.sec.gov/files/marketstructure/research/dera_wp_the_effect_of_amendment_10_of_the_luld_plan.pdf)
- [PEER] Hautsch & Horvath, "How effective are trading pauses?" JFE 131 (2019; online 2017/18), Nasdaq order-book data, difference-in-differences: pauses improve price discovery during the break but have adverse effects on price stability and liquidity after the pause; they "do not cool off markets but cause extra volatility"; traders also change behavior in anticipation of a pause. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X18302356); [SUFE abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/355951)
- [WP, Japan] A recent Tokyo Stock Exchange working paper finds the opposite for Tokyo (pauses reduce post-event volatility and spreads), but benefits are attenuated for highly volatile stocks with recent negative returns; design differs from Hautsch & Horvath. Search results pointed to [JPX Working Paper 52](https://www.jpx.co.jp/english/corporate/research-study/working-paper/JPXWP_Vol52e.pdf) and [Osaka DP 2603](https://www2.econ.osaka-u.ac.jp/econ_society/dp/2603.pdf); which one is the source could not be confirmed.
- [VENDOR/unverified] A broker blog cites an unnamed study of volatility pauses in "pump" moves where the reopening price exceeded the prior high by 10% on average (bad for shorts); authors, sample and date not identifiable. — [fondexx.pro](https://fondexx.pro/blog/what-trading-halt-and-why-it-matters-investors-and-traders)
- [VENDOR] Trading-education sites claim each successive halt in the same direction lowers continuation odds and three or more up-halts raise reversal odds; no supporting study found. (The search extract did not say which site made the claim; halt-strategy guides in the results included [finwiz](https://finwiz.io/day-trading/halt-trading-strategy) and [EdgeTick](https://edgetick.com/trading-halts-explained-luld-t12/).)
- [PEER, China, different rule] Chinese daily price-limit hits: continuation more likely than reversal after limit-up and limit-down days - a different market and mechanism. — [arXiv 1503.03548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.03548)
- [REG] SEC's January 2010 Concept Release on Equity Market Structure (File S7-02-10) opened review of high-frequency and potentially manipulative strategies; the exact "momentum ignition" wording could not be retrieved in this session. — [Federal Register, 75 FR 3594](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2010/01/21/2010-1045/concept-release-on-equity-market-structure)
- [PEER] Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw, "Maxing Out," JFE 99, 2011: stocks with the highest maximum daily return in the prior month underperform; low-MAX minus high-MAX decile return differences exceed 1% per month (raw and risk-adjusted). — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-off-the-jfe-press-maxing-out-your-returns/). A 2021 paper argues post-high-MAX underperformance reflects overreaction embedded in the extreme return rather than lottery preference. — [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/it-could-be-overreaction-not-lottery-seeking-that-is-behind-bali-/)
- [PEER] Hong, Scheinkman & Xiong, "Asset Float and Speculative Bubbles," J. Finance 61(3), 2006: with short-sale constraints and overconfident investors, a small float produces a larger bubble, higher turnover and volatility; prices drop when float expands (lockup expiry). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v61y2006i3p1073-1117.html); [NBER WP 11367](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w11367/w11367.pdf)
- [REG/exchange] Nasdaq (Sept 3, 2025) proposed higher float and IPO-proceeds minimums (e.g. $15M market value of unrestricted publicly held shares under the net-income standard, up from $8M) and faster suspension/delisting for deficient companies under $5M market value, citing "emerging patterns associated with potential pump-and-dump schemes"; approval status not confirmed. — [Nasdaq press release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/nasdaq-proposes-changes-its-listing-standards-2025-09-03); [Mayer Brown](https://acrosstheboard.mayerbrown.com/2025/09/nasdaq-rule-proposal-seeks-enhanced-float-and-ipo-listing-requirements-faster-delisting-for-non-compliant-companies/)

### Inferences
- LULD pauses in small stocks are frequently liquidity-gap events, and post-pause liquidity is worse (Hautsch & Horvath). A halt-resumption buy therefore faces wide, unstable reopening prices; any edge must clear unusually high slippage. Nothing found shows it does.
- The low-float bubble model (Hong et al.) gives a market reason why low-float runners can rip far past value (constrained shorting plus speculation) and why they collapse when supply arrives (offerings, warrant exercise, lockups). That supports trading them only as short-lived momentum with hard exits, never as holds.
- MAX/lottery and no-news-shock evidence both say extreme up-moves are, on average, overpriced afterwards. Buying a parabolic burst is buying the input to a documented reversal; any edge would have to come from catalyst quality and very short holding times.
- The 2025 Nasdaq actions confirm that a meaningful share of extreme low-float IPO runners in recent years were suspected manipulation; a "momentum ignition"-looking burst in a micro-float with no real news is a pump risk, not a signal.

### Gaps
- No US study of returns after LULD halt resumption for small caps (continuation vs reversal, by halt count or direction) was found.
- The SEC's exact definition of momentum ignition, and any study of its frequency in low-float names, could not be retrieved.
- No study of intraday "speed bursts" (e.g. 5%+ in one minute) and their next-minutes returns in small caps was found.

## 5. Intraday momentum literature and what it implies for these names

### Takeaway
Documented intraday momentum is mainly a market/ETF phenomenon (first half-hour predicts last half-hour) and a daily-lag phenomenon in single stocks; within the day, single-stock half-hour returns tend to reverse. Overnight-vs-intraday research says retail-attention stocks open too high and reverse intraday. News-driven moves drift over days to weeks. Taken together, the evidence favours selective, catalyst-backed longs held for short periods with tight cost control, and does not support a generic "momentum continues intraday" assumption for small caps.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER] Gao, Han, Li & Zhou, "Market Intraday Momentum," JFE 129, 2018: on SPY 1993-2013, the first half-hour return (from the previous close) predicts the last half-hour return; stronger on volatile days, high-volume days, recession days and macro-news days; also in ten other heavily traded ETFs; consistent with infrequent rebalancing and late-informed trading. — [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)
- Critique: the economic-significance tests ignore trading frictions, and testing many segment combinations adds data-snooping bias. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=25481)
- [PEER] Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka 2010: within-day half-hour reversal; continuation only at multiples of one day (same time of day on later days). — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1005.3535)
- [WP, secondary] A thesis summarizing Kang (2005) on NYSE stocks reports that large caps reverse after the first ~90 minutes while small stocks continue momentum for the whole day; not verified against the original. — [Tilburg thesis](http://arno.uvt.nl/show.cgi?fid=144554)
- [PEER] Berkman et al. 2012 and Aboody et al. 2018 (see section 1): high-attention stocks' overnight gains reverse intraday; overnight returns persist for weeks then reverse over a year.
- [PEER] Jiang & Zhu 2017, Chan 2003, Savor 2012 (see section 1): news-driven jumps drift over 1-3 months; no-news shocks reverse.
- [WP] Kargarzadeh et al. 2026 (see section 1): most of the news move is done by the close of day 0.

### Inferences
- For news runners, the strongest documented continuation is across days (post-news drift), not within the morning; the strongest documented intraday effect for attention stocks is reversal from the open.
- The Gao et al. effect is strongest on high-volatility, high-volume, news days - the conditions under which low-float runners also move - but it was measured on SPY/ETFs with no costs, so it does not transfer directly.
- If Kang's small-stock continuation finding holds, it would support holding a working long into the day rather than scalping; this is the one piece pointing that way and it is unverified.

### Gaps
- No intraday-momentum study on a $1-$20, low-float, news-driven universe was found.
- No intraday post-news drift study at minute resolution for small caps (e.g. "news at 8:00, how do returns 9:30-11:00 behave?") was found.
- Overnight-vs-intraday split for small caps specifically (as opposed to all stocks) was not found in accessible sources (Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019 on overnight vs intraday returns was not checked).

## 6. Retail day-trading outcome studies: what share profit, and what distinguished the few who did

### Takeaway
Every large dataset found shows that most day traders lose money after costs and only a very small share are reliably profitable: under 1% predictably profitable in Taiwan's complete market data (1992-2006), 97% losing among persistent Brazilian futures day traders, and roughly 20% more-than-marginally profitable in a US late-1990s sample. The minority who profit show persistence, trade where information asymmetry is high (around news, in small/volatile stocks), and earn gross profits mostly with aggressive orders - but these traits are shared by many losers too. The best-known "low-float momentum" educator settled FTC charges over earnings claims, with the FTC finding most customers lost money.

### Cited Findings
- [PEER] Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean, "The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill: Evidence from Day Trading," J. Financial Markets 2014, Taiwan 1992-2006: less than 1% of the day-trader population predictably and reliably earns positive abnormal returns net of fees. The top 500 traders (ranked on the prior year) go on to earn 61.3 bps/day before fees, 37.9 bps after; bottom-ranked earn -11.5 bps before and -28.9 bps after (working-paper figures differ: 49.5 / 28.1 bps). — [eScholarship](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/7k75v0qx); [Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/The%20Cross-Section%20of%20Speculator%20Skill.pdf); [2011 working paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- [WP] Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean, "Do Individual Day Traders Make Money? Evidence from Taiwan" (2004): in a typical six-month period more than eight in ten day traders lose money; heavy day traders earn gross profits that do not cover costs; past winners stay winners more often (65.9% of the top partition profitable again next period) while 97% of the worst group lose again; profitable traders appear to profit from short-term forecasting when information asymmetry is high (around earnings) or in hard-to-value (small, volatile) stocks. About 1% of individuals account for half of day-trading volume (a volume share, not a profitability figure). — [Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trade%20040330.pdf); [Yale copy](http://www.econ.yale.edu/~shiller/behfin/2004-04-10/barber-lee-liu-odean.pdf)
- [WP/PEER] Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean & Zhang, "Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability?" / "Learning, Fast or Slow" (2020): day traders keep trading despite losses; experience does not raise profits (per secondary summaries); a summary reports average net losses of about 23.9 bps per day. — [Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trading%20and%20Learning%20110217.pdf); [Tradicted summary](https://www.tradicted.com/research/barber-learning-2020/)
- [WP] Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (FGV WP 525 / SSRN 3423101, rev. 2020): all individuals who began day trading Brazil's mini-index futures in 2013-2015 and persisted at least 300 days: 97% lost money; only 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage; 0.5% (0.4% in one version) more than a bank teller's starting salary; the top earner averaged about $310/day with a daily standard deviation of $2,560; no evidence of improvement with time; HFT presence hurt individuals but did not explain the lack of learning. Caveat: futures, one country, possibly hard period. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fgv:eesptd:525); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/analysis/new-study-shows-just-how-unprofitable-day-trading-is/amp); [CXO Advisory caveats](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/day-trading-a-bust/)
- [PEER] Jordan & Diltz, "The Profitability of Day Traders," FAJ 59(6), 2003, US accounts: about twice as many day traders lose money as make money; about 20% were more than marginally profitable; profitability tracked Nasdaq Composite moves (late-1990s bubble sample; the "334 accounts, Feb 1998-Oct 1999, 35% profitable" details come from a secondary source, unverified). — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2003/the-profitability-of-day-traders); [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v59y2003i6p85-94.html)
- [WP/PEER] Garvey & Murphy, "The Profitability of Active Stock Traders": 1,386 day traders at a US direct-access broker, 400,000+ trades, March 8 - June 13, 2000: about half were profitable after commissions; winners made $9.5M net intraday, losers lost $4.6M; three months is too short to separate luck from skill (and the period was the dot-com peak). — [Oxford-hosted PDF](https://www.nuff.ox.ac.uk/Users/MurphyA/Active%20Traders.pdf); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/the-odds-of-winning-as-an-active-trader/)
- [REG] FTC v. Warrior Trading and CEO Ross Cameron (April 2022): $3 million consumer redress and a ban on unsubstantiated earnings claims, no admission of wrongdoing; the FTC cited claims such as "Learn How I Made over $101,280.47 in Verified Profits Day Trading Part Time in Under 45 Days" (Jan 2018 - Mar 2021 marketing) and said most customers lost money trading on top of what they paid for the programs. — [FTC business blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2022/04/ftc-bids-good-night-deceptive-day-trading-earnings-claims); [FTC consumer alert](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2022/04/day-trading-earnings-werent-payday-warrior-trading-promised); [Frankfurt Kurnit summary](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102hn6f/ftc-gets-3m-settlement-over-false-money-making-claims)
- [PEER/WP] Robinhood herding study (section 1): the retail crowd as a whole lost about 5% relative to the market in each herding episode. — [UC Davis summary](https://gsm.ucdavis.edu/news/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market)

### Inferences
- The base rate for discretionary day trading is strongly negative; any claimed edge in low-float momentum should be treated as unproven until it shows up in the bot's own live fills, net of costs, across many trades and days.
- What the profitable minority have in common (persistence; trading around information events in small, volatile stocks; taking liquidity decisively) overlaps with the owner's playbook, which is mildly encouraging - but the same studies show most people trying it lose, so the filters (catalyst, relative volume, leaders only) and cost control carry the weight.
- Results from dot-com-era samples (Jordan & Diltz; Garvey & Murphy) are the most favourable and the most period-dependent; the complete-market Taiwan and Brazil datasets, which avoid self-selection by broker, are the more reliable base rates.
- Course/vendor performance claims in this niche are legally contested marketing (FTC 2022) and should not be used as evidence.

### Gaps
- No study of US retail traders specifically trading sub-$20 low-float news runners was found (the Taiwan and Brazil data cover all stocks / index futures).
- The characteristics separating the <1% persistent winners (e.g. holding time, stock type, time of day) are only partially described in accessible abstracts; the full Barber et al. tables were not read.
- No audited, third-party performance record of any low-float momentum educator was found.
