# Costs Swallow the Small-Cap Momentum Edge

**No public study shows a cost-surviving edge in buying low-float news gappers during the day, and the best evidence points against the exact entry these bots take.** Peer-reviewed work finds that stocks hit by a burst of retail attention open too high and give back during the day, and that extreme one-day winners do worse afterwards. Vendor and trader counts say about **two of every three stocks that gap 50% or more close below their open**. The strongest public intraday backtest, the "Stocks in Play" opening-range breakout, is a working paper co-written by a man who sells day-trading education. Its edge comes almost entirely from picking the day's most unusually active stocks. It is thin per trade (a reported +0.08R for stocks trading above normal volume, +0.38R only for the most extreme volume), and it leaves out stocks under $5. Real news does push prices for weeks, but that drift is measured close to close, not from a chased premarket print. Premarket spreads run a reported **3-4 times** wider than in regular hours, and price impact is at least as large a multiple. Alpaca accepts only limit orders there, and no circuit breaker limits the moves, which explains stops that fill 15c or more below their price. The short side has the better win rate, but the strongest studies say borrow fees, spreads and squeezes eat the gross edge, and most of these names cannot be shorted on Alpaca anyway. A small bot can still improve: fewer trades, only top-ranked names with real news, no paying over the ask, a spread check before each buy, and rules judged on tick-and-quote replays and hundreds of live trades. It cannot realistically win a speed race into 5-second bursts, and it should not trust any vendor's win rate. The researchers read search-engine extracts, not full papers, so every figure below is "reported" and should be checked against the original.

## Read every number as "reported," and weigh it by its grade

Web page fetching was blocked for the research team. Every figure here comes from search-engine extracts of abstracts, summaries, listings and vendor pages. The direction of each finding is reasonably safe. The exact numbers are not, and several sources disagree with each other; where they do, this report says so. Each claim carries one tag from the table below.

| Tag | What it is | How much weight it carries |
|---|---|---|
| [Peer-reviewed] | Published in a refereed journal | Strongest. But most of these studies cover all stocks, daily or monthly horizons and regular hours, not $1-$20 low-float premarket names. |
| [Working paper] | Preprint, thesis, unrefereed paper | Medium. Not yet checked by referees; numbers can change. |
| [Regulator] | SEC, FINRA, exchange or LULD Plan rules and data | Strong for rules and counts; studies are often several years old. |
| [Broker docs] | Alpaca documentation, staff forum answers, Alpaca's own filings | Good for how the broker works; says nothing about market edges. |
| [Vendor] | A firm selling data, scanners, courses or a strategy | Weak. Method hidden, money at stake. |
| [Practitioner] | A trader's own published backtest or record | Weak to medium. Some method shown; small or self-selected. |
| [Anecdote] | One case, a press story, a claim with no method | Illustration only. |
| [Own log] | This project's paper fills and replays, in `memory/journal.md` | Most relevant, but few days, and paper fills. |
| [Arithmetic] | A calculation by the researchers, not from a source | Reproducible; check the inputs. |

Four biases run through this field, and a reader should watch for each one. The first is **money at stake**. SmallCapLab, the main source of fade statistics below, sells a systematic small-cap short strategy ([SmallCapLab](https://www.smallcaplab.com/data)). Andrew Aziz, co-author of the best-known intraday paper, runs the Bear Bull Traders day-trading community that promotes it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Aziz)). Warrior Trading and its CEO Ross Cameron agreed to **$3 million in consumer redress** to settle FTC charges over earnings claims, without admitting wrongdoing; the FTC said most customers lost money trading on top of what they paid for the courses [Regulator] ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2022/04/ftc-bids-good-night-deceptive-day-trading-earnings-claims)). The second is **survivorship**: a backtest that uses only stocks listed today drops the ones that failed. One study of India's small-cap index found that a current-members backtest silently left out **1,185 stocks** [Working paper] ([arXiv 2603.19380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19380)). None of the public gapper samples below says whether delisted tickers are included, and one tick-data seller admits that "very thinly traded names and short-lived shell tickers are often not in the historical archive" [Vendor] ([Kibot FAQ](https://www.kibot.com/faq.html)). That is exactly the low-float universe.

The third bias is **gross versus net**. Concretum Group's paywalled fade strategy reports about a 98% yearly growth rate with a 67% maximum drawdown, before costs [Vendor] ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/identifying-stocks-to-fade)). A university thesis testing gap reversal on US stocks from 2011 to 2024 found a large gross edge that turned **clearly negative after spreads and commissions**, and the reversal was strongest in small, illiquid stocks [Working paper] ([Turku thesis](https://www.utupub.fi/bitstreams/88057d2a-1ee8-4c05-9592-c535a21b502c/download)). The fourth is **tuning on the same data**. Trade-Ideas listed its "Bullish Pullback" algorithm at a 79.3% win rate in one 2018 post and 71.9% two days earlier [Vendor] ([Trade-Ideas](https://www.trade-ideas.com/2018/07/26/bullish-pullback-leads-20-algos-16-long-holly-morning-huddle/)). A TradingView short signal advertises an 81.8% win rate built on 166 gappers over 70 days [Vendor] ([TradingView](https://kr.tradingview.com/script/WuHhPb6z-Gapper-SHORT-Signal)). Numbers like these are fitted, not found.

One more caution applies to the bots themselves. Their "live" results are **Alpaca paper fills on live quotes**, not real-money fills ([journal](../memory/journal.md)). The section on premarket costs explains why paper flatters a strategy that trades size in thin names.

## Stock selection shows a thin edge; buying the burst shows none

### Long setups: the open of an attention stock is a documented bad price

No peer-reviewed study or cost-inclusive public backtest was found for the core long trade: buying the premarket-high break or the open on a day-one low-float news gapper. The closest rigorous evidence points against it. Berkman, Koch, Tuttle and Zhang found that stocks which recently drew retail attention earn positive overnight returns and then **reverse during the trading day**, because the opening price is set too high by retail buying at the first chance to buy. The effect is stronger in hard-to-value, costly-to-arbitrage stocks and when retail sentiment runs hot. For retail traders buying such stocks near the open, the hidden cost reportedly "frequently exceed[s] the effective half spread" [Peer-reviewed] ([JFQA 2012, IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v47y2012i04p715-741_00.html)). Barber, Huang, Odean and Schwarz found that the stocks Robinhood users piled into each day earned average five-day abnormal returns of about **-3%**, and about **-6%** for the most extreme herding [Peer-reviewed; figures from drafts] ([SSRN draft](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/SSRN-id3715077.pdf)). They tied the herding to the app's "Top Movers" list, which is a scanner by another name ([UC Davis](https://gsm.ucdavis.edu/news/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market)).

A high fade rate is not a free short, and a high continuation rate would not be a free long. One trader tested fading QQQ gaps above 2% and lost money: a profit factor of 0.65, with only **39.6% of 275 trades** profitable, because the stop was hit before the gap filled [Practitioner] ([Kai Yasuda](https://kaiyasuda.substack.com/p/the-gap-fill-teardown)). Base rates and stops interact, and only a test with the actual stop can tell whether a pattern pays.

The best support for the long side is news, and it runs over days, not minutes. Chan found that stocks with real headline news keep drifting in the news direction, while similar moves without news partly reverse, mostly in smaller stocks [Peer-reviewed] ([JFE 2003 abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357702)). Savor found the same split using analyst reports: **big moves with information drift, big moves without information reverse** [Peer-reviewed] ([JFE 2012](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p635-659.html)). Jiang and Zhu found that stocks jumping up on news keep outperforming for one to three months, overnight jumps included [Peer-reviewed] ([JFE 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v124y2017i1p43-64.html)). A 2026 study of 4.57 million news articles adds a warning. Most of the news move is done by the close of the publication day; pooled, the day-0 move is reportedly about **2.8 times** its size twenty days later. Launch and partnership releases, the typical low-float catalyst, appeared to overshoot, and a rule fading small-cap launch and partnership news earned a reported 15.8% a year **before costs**. The authors call this descriptive, and a robustness check pushed the launch estimate across zero [Working paper] ([arXiv 2608.14014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14014); [review](https://pith.science/paper/2608.14014)). The lesson is that the long edge, if any, sits in the quality of the catalyst, not in paying the first print.

The Stocks-in-Play opening-range breakout paper by Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz is the strongest public intraday stock backtest. It tested a 5-minute opening range on 7,000+ US stocks from 2016 to 2023 and reported a top-20 portfolio return above **1,600% net**, 41.6% a year, Sharpe 2.81, near-zero market beta [Working paper, author-affiliated] ([SSRN 4729284](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4729284)). Four facts limit how far it transfers. Its universe, per a secondary summary, requires a price above $5, average volume above 1 million shares and a daily range above $0.50, which excludes most $1-$5 low-float names ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/stocks-in-play-trading-strategy-day-trading/)). It trades long and short together, and no long-only result was found. Its edge depends on selection: trades with relative volume under 1x averaged a reported **-0.02R**, above 1x **+0.08R**, and above 30x **+0.38R** ([QuantifiedStrategies Substack](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/stocks-in-play-opening-range-breakout)). A critic notes that plain opening-range breakout on liquid stocks made about 29% over the whole period, worse than buying and holding, and that "selecting the day's most unusually active stocks... did almost all the work" [Practitioner] ([danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)). Finally, replications elsewhere follow a familiar pattern. A QuantConnect re-run on liquid stocks reported a Sharpe near 2.4 [Practitioner] ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)), but a replication of the sibling QQQ version on index CFDs reproduced the gross result and found the net result "indistinguishable from zero" [Practitioner] ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). The earlier QQQ paper assumed no slippage and its 9,350% figure came from a parameter search ([danfin.net](https://danfin.net/opening-range-breakout-research)). A pre-registered futures study is titled "Opening-Range Breakout Does Not Survive Trading Costs" (title only seen) [Working paper] ([SSRN 7428398](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7428398)).

The cost arithmetic is unforgiving. At +0.08R a trade, costs equal to 8% of the stop distance wipe out the edge. With a 10-cent stop on a $4 stock, two cents of bad fill on each side is already 40% of R [Arithmetic]. Only the extreme relative-volume bucket leaves room for real costs, which supports the owner's "#1 and #2 leaders first" rule more than any other single finding here.

No study compares buying the first breakout with buying the first pullback, a VWAP reclaim or a bull flag in news-driven small caps. The available numbers are daily-chart pattern counts, such as a reported 55% failure rate for bull flags across 1,028 cases, seen only through a secondary site [Anecdote] ([Liberated Stock Trader](https://liberatedstocktrader.com/?p=24413)), and vendor win rates. One rigorous, related fact exists: within the day, a stock's half-hour return tends to **reverse** the previous half-hours, while it continues at the same time of day on later days [Peer-reviewed] ([Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka 2010](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1005.3535)). That is a weak, indirect argument against paying for the top of a spike. Order aggressiveness does not settle it either: in Taiwan's full market records, the few profitable heavy day traders made a reported **83% of their gross profits** with aggressive orders, but nearly all individual losses also came from aggressive orders [Working paper] ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean 2004](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trade%20040330.pdf)). What separated winners from losers was what they bought, not how they clicked.

Buying parabolic bursts and halt resumptions has no documented positive expectancy. The SEC's staff found that after a trading pause, prices typically returned to the bands in force before the pause, and that some pauses come from temporary liquidity gaps that revert once liquidity returns [Regulator] ([SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-luld-white-paper.pdf)). More than half of all pauses occurred in just 50 of over 5,000 symbols, mostly small, low-volume stocks [Regulator] ([LULD assessment](https://cdn.luldplan.com/reports/LULD-Supplemental-Joint-Assessment.pdf)). Pauses "do not cool off markets but cause extra volatility," and liquidity is worse after them [Peer-reviewed] ([Hautsch & Horvath, JFE 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X18302356)). Stocks with the highest one-day return in a month underperform afterwards by more than 1% a month versus the lowest [Peer-reviewed] ([Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw, via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-off-the-jfe-press-maxing-out-your-returns/)), most likely from overreaction ([Gorman et al., Critical Finance Review](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0123)).

The intraday-momentum literature does not rescue the long burst. The well-known finding that the first half-hour predicts the last half-hour was measured on SPY and other big ETFs, without trading costs [Peer-reviewed] ([Gao, Han, Li & Zhou](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)), and critics note that it ignores frictions and tested many combinations ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=25481)). One older NYSE study, seen only through a student thesis, reports that small stocks keep their momentum all day while large ones reverse; it is the one piece of evidence favouring holding a working long, and it is unverified [Working paper, secondary] ([Tilburg thesis](http://arno.uvt.nl/show.cgi?fid=144554)).

### Short setups: high win rates, unproven net profit, often not available

The short fade looks better on paper. About two-thirds of 50%+ gappers close below their open (section four). The academic evidence agrees that hard-to-short, attention-driven stocks earn low returns later. But three findings cut the gross edge down. Across 162 known anomalies, average long-short returns were about **0.14-0.15% a month before short-sale costs and about zero or slightly negative after**, with all the profit in the short leg; the sample also left out stocks under $1 or $50 million in value [Peer-reviewed] ([Muravyev, Pearson & Pollet, via CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/short-selling/shorting-costs-kill-stock-return-anomalies)). Major anomalies, the "extreme winner" effect among them, "effectively disappear" in the 80% of stocks that are cheap to borrow and are amplified in expensive ones [Working paper] ([Drechsler & Drechsler, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20282)). And loan fees jump while loan supply shrinks after extreme past returns, "precisely when it is costliest for a short-seller" [Peer-reviewed] ([Engelberg, Reed & Ringgenberg](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/riggenberg.pdf)).

Live short records are weaker than short backtests. One practitioner who backtested 3,636 big gappers since 2008 reported a March 2025 account loss of $4,350, as tradable gaps fell from 74 to 45 a month, and later wrote that "all three of my signals made the same core bet," so they failed together [Practitioner] ([Stonks Capital](https://stonkscapital.substack.com/p/march-2025-recap); [follow-up](https://stonkscapital.substack.com/p/the-day-all-my-uncorrelated-signals)). A large-cap overnight-gap reversal study did report 51.47% a year after costs, but on S&P 500 stocks, which says nothing about micro-floats [Peer-reviewed] ([Stübinger & Schneider](https://open.fau.de/handle/openfau/11837)). The payoff is also lopsided: many modest wins and rare 100%+ squeezes, such as DryShips' 2,000%+ run in 2016 [Anecdote] ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/amp/content/8719057)).

Access is the final block. Alpaca opens shorts only in easy-to-borrow stocks, cancels a pending short that turns hard-to-borrow overnight, and charges and can recall existing shorts that become hard to borrow [Broker docs] ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/margin-and-short-selling)). Alpaca has announced hard-to-borrow shorting with API locates, but its launch date and fee schedule were not confirmed [Broker docs] ([Alpaca blog](https://alpaca.markets/blog/htb-trading-api-locates/)). The short-sale circuit breaker (Rule 201) triggers at a 10% drop from the prior close, so it rarely binds on day one of a gap-up; it matters on day two [Regulator] ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2010/34-61595fr.pdf)).

### Most people who try this lose money

The base rate frames everything else. In Taiwan's complete market records from 1992 to 2006, **less than 1% of day traders** predictably earned positive returns after fees in the published version (an earlier draft says fewer than 3%) [Peer-reviewed] ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean 2014](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/7k75v0qx)). Of Brazilians who day-traded index futures for at least 300 days, **97% lost money** and only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage [Working paper] ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). A US sample from the late-1990s bubble found about twice as many losers as winners, with about 20% more than marginally profitable [Peer-reviewed] ([Jordan & Diltz](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2003/the-profitability-of-day-traders)). Taiwan's few winners traded where information gaps were large, around news and in small, volatile stocks ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean 2004](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/Day%20Traders/Day%20Trade%20040330.pdf)). That overlaps the owner's playbook, which is mildly encouraging, but most people doing the same thing still lost.

| Strategy | Best evidence found | Grade | Cost-surviving edge? |

| Opening-range breakout on top relative-volume stocks | Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz 2024; one independent re-run | Working paper, author-affiliated | Reported yes for liquid stocks over $5; thin per trade; untested under $5 or in low floats |

| First pullback, VWAP reclaim, bull flag | Daily-chart counts; vendor win rates | Anecdote, Vendor | Unproven either way |
| Buy halt resumptions or parabolic bursts | Pause data; extreme-winner reversal; half-hour reversal | Regulator, Peer-reviewed (indirect) | Evidence against |
| Hold news-backed moves for days | Chan, Savor, Jiang & Zhu | Peer-reviewed | Real drift, but close to close over weeks |

## Premarket fills cost three to four times more, with no safety net

### Thin books and informed traders set the premarket price

Every measured study finds trading outside regular hours costs a multiple of daytime trading. Barclay and Hendershott found **less than one-twentieth as many trades** per unit of time after hours, and **quoted and effective spreads 3-4 times larger**, which they traced to more informed traders on the other side, not to dealer profits [Peer-reviewed] ([JF 2004 summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/361191)). Their data are from Nasdaq in 1999-2000, when prices moved in sixteenths. A Nasdaq article cites the same work as "4-5 times" the cost [Regulator] ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/looking-all-day-data-24-hour-trading)). Newer work on the 8 p.m.-4 a.m. session finds price impact "at least three to four times greater" than in regular hours; Nasdaq's summary says about 6 times, and versions of the paper differ [Working paper] ([Eaton, Shkilko & Werner](https://ssrn.com/abstract=5181159)). The SEC warns that most stocks may have no active market maker after hours [Regulator] ([SEC bulletin](https://www.sec.gov/files/afterhourtrading.pdf)).

The premarket is still where the information arrives. Pre-open trades carry more information per trade than daytime trades [Peer-reviewed] ([Barclay & Hendershott 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v16y2003i4p1041-1073.html)), but pre-open trading needs a threshold of volume before it adds anything to the opening price [Peer-reviewed] ([Barclay & Hendershott 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v15y2008i5p839-849.html)). **A premarket high printed on light volume is a weak level.** Premarket volume is growing fast: extended hours reached 11.5% of all US stock trading in Q2 2025, with premarket 59% of it [Regulator] ([NYSE](https://beta.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/NYSE_Research_Q2_2025_Extended_Hours_Trading_Review.pdf)). Stocks under $1 grew from 5% to almost 20% of premarket volume between early 2023 and late 2024 [Regulator] ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/early-birds-and-night-owls-how-extended-trading-hours-are-reshaping-u-s-equities-markets-/)). No study was found that measures premarket spreads or depth for low-priced small caps. The 3-4x figure is a floor borrowed from bigger stocks.

### Limit-only orders and missing circuit breakers explain the slipped stops

At Alpaca, extended-hours orders must be limit orders with the extended-hours flag set; stop and stop-limit orders are rejected [Broker docs] ([Alpaca orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/orders-at-alpaca); [forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/allowing-premarket-stop-limit-orders/18645)). A premarket stop is therefore two steps the bot builds itself: watch the price, then send a limit sell. **A sell limited at the stop price does not fill when the bid gaps below it**, and the position stays open while the price falls. A sell priced below the bid fills, but in a thin book it can sweep several price levels ([Achievable, FINRA exam prep](https://achievable.me/exams/finra-sie/insights/stop-orders-avoid-costly-trading-mistakes/)). Limit Up-Limit Down bands run only from 9:30 to 4:00, so a premarket stock can move 50-100% in minutes with no pause [Regulator] ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/guardrails-market-volatility)). The rule for cancelling "clearly erroneous" trades in stocks up to $25 widens from 10% in regular hours to **20% premarket**, so a print 10-19% away from the last trade stands [Regulator] ([Nasdaq](https://nasdaqtrader.com/trader.aspx?id=ceform)). Protection against trading through a better price on another exchange covers regular hours only, by an informal account [Broker docs, forum] ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/extend-hours-trading-development/9380); [FlexTrade](https://flextrade.com/order-protection-rule/)), and in June 2026 the SEC proposed rescinding that rule altogether; the proposal is not final ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/sitecore/content/whlaunch/home/research/news-publications/20260617-the-sec-takes-aim-at-the-trade-through-rule)).

The bots' own record shows these mechanics at work. On 10-08, **12 trades had the bid already under the stop at the moment of the fill, costing $1,519**, because a fast buy paid up to the ask plus 20c while the stop sat 10c under the fill. BIAF paid $7.49 with the bid at $6.82 and was sold 8 milliseconds later. Forty-six trades took over two seconds or several orders to sell, as premarket limit sells were cancelled and re-sent about once a second [Own log] ([journal](../memory/journal.md)). The "fills 15c+ below stops" problem is therefore not bad luck. It is the predictable result of three known facts: a stop narrower than the spread, limit-only orders, and a book with little on the bid.

Regular hours bring their own trap. Small stocks priced above $3 get 10% bands and those from $0.75 to $3 get 20%, doubled from 3:35 to 4:00 for stocks at or below $3. If a quote sits on a band for 15 seconds, all trading pauses for 5 minutes, extendable in 5-minute steps, and the stock reopens in an auction [Regulator] ([Nasdaq LULD FAQ](https://m.nasdaqtrader.com/content/MarketRegulation/LULD_FAQ.pdf); [Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/document/tech-spec/content/technical-specifications/cboe-limit-updown-faq/luld-amendment-18-update)). Nothing can be sold during a pause. A disproportionate share of pauses comes in the first 15 minutes of the session [Regulator] ([SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/dera/staff-papers/white-papers/10mar17moiseflahertyluld)).

### Order type trades price for selection

Limit orders at or better than the quote beat market orders on average, even after counting the orders that never fill [Peer-reviewed] ([Harris & Hasbrouck](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v31y1996i02p213-231_00.html)). The catch is **adverse selection**: a resting limit order fills when better-informed traders are on the other side [Peer-reviewed] ([Linnainmaa 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i4p1473-1506.html)). On a breakout, a passive buy below the market fills mostly when the breakout fails and misses the runners. It improves the price of the trades it gets and worsens which trades it gets. Marketable limit orders look costlier than market orders in raw averages only because traders use them in fast, wide markets [Peer-reviewed] ([Peterson & Sirri](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v37y2002i02p221-241_00.html)).

In regular hours, retail market orders routed to wholesalers get real price improvement, a reported **24% of the quoted spread** overall [Peer-reviewed] ([Dyhrberg, Shkilko & Werner](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=13580)). The same small market order placed at five brokers cost **0.07% to 0.46%** round trip depending on the account [Peer-reviewed] ([Schwarz et al., JF 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v80y2025i5p2507-2541.html)). No source showed similar price improvement premarket, and Nasdaq notes that daytime protections and execution reporting "are not in force during extended hours" ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/looking-all-day-data-24-hour-trading)). Alpaca routes to wholesalers and is paid by them; its own filing says payment and price improvement come "from the same pool" [Broker docs] ([Alpaca 606](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/SEC+606a1+-+2026Q2.pdf)).

### Paper fills flatter size and passive orders

Alpaca's paper trading fills against the live best bid and ask, but it does not check order size against the size shown at the quote, does not model queue position or market impact, and adds random partial fills about 10% of the time [Broker docs] ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/alpaca-paper-trading-partial-order-fill/2683); [Alpaca paper docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading); [TradersPost](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/alpaca-paper-trading)). One user's buy of more than twice a stock's traded volume filled in full. Alpaca's own learning material calls paper fills "idealized" ([Alpaca Learn](https://alpaca.markets/learn/paper-trading-vs-live-trading-a-data-backed-guide-on-when-to-start-trading-real-money)). In a premarket book showing a few hundred shares at the ask, a real order for a few thousand shares would sweep several levels or fill in part. **Real-money results on these names are likely to be worse than the paper results**, not better.

The bots' cost numbers already tell the story. Each trade pays about **0.6% in spread** on these stocks. Over three days, the spread plus paying over the ask came to roughly **70-80% of v36 and v36b's losses** and more than v37's whole loss. Replayed at its stop prices, BIAF would have cost v36 $157 instead of $516: "the damage was the fills." The replay is kinder than live: one morning's replay showed +$1,201 at fills 0.2% worse, while live lost about $734, putting live costs near 0.5-1% [Own log] ([journal](../memory/journal.md)). One practitioner guide puts slippage for $1-$10 small caps at 25-100 basis points per side and calls the common 5-basis-point backtest default "fiction" [Practitioner] ([skills.cat](https://skills.cat/skills/jefrnc/quant-llm-skills/transaction-cost-modeling)). The rules are also shifting: Cboe targets near-24-hour trading on EDGX in December 2026 [Regulator] ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/expanding-market-access-why-cboe-plans-to-launch-near-24-x-5-u-s-equities-trading-this-year/)), and the LULD committee intends to add overnight price bands ([LULD 2025 report](https://cdn.luldplan.com/reports/LULD-2025-Annual-Report.pdf)).

## Two of three big gappers close below their open

### The typical day-one shape: a push, a deep dip, a red close

No peer-reviewed or regulator dataset measures how US low-float gappers behave within day one. Two large non-academic samples agree on the direction. SmallCapLab tracks about 2,999 stocks that gapped 50% or more since January 2022. On its running averages, **65.6% closed below their regular-hours open**, only **3.9%** gave back the whole gap, and the average low of day was **22.2% below the open**. Only **42.2%** ever traded above their premarket high in regular hours, and 14.1% broke the premarket high and still closed below the open. The median move from the open to the high of day was about **34%**, and larger gaps "extend further intraday but also collapse harder" [Vendor] ([SmallCapLab](https://www.smallcaplab.com/research)). The page showed some placeholder-looking values, its method could not be read, and the firm sells a short strategy. A trader using a different data source counted 3,685 stocks gapping above 50% since 2008 and found **70.8% closed red** [Practitioner] ([Stonks Capital](https://stonkscapital.substack.com/p/why-i-short-small-caps-even-though)). He sorts the outcomes into three groups: fades from the open, squeezes of varying size, and stocks that run into the close ([Stonks Capital](https://stonkscapital.substack.com/p/the-science-of-shorting-using-backtesting)).

Read together, these say the long money on day one is in timing the first push, not in holding into the afternoon. They also say a premarket-high break alone is a weak signal: fewer than half break it, and about a third of those still end red [Vendor, arithmetic on vendor figures]. No public numbers exist for 20%+ gaps, for float or price buckets, or for the time of the high of day.

### Five-second bursts dip before they run, and most look-alikes never come back

No public study measures what happens in the minutes after a 5%-in-seconds burst in a small cap. The closest published facts are indirect: half-hour returns tend to reverse within the day (Heston et al., above), and the average big gapper trades about 22% below its open at some point (vendor, above). On these names, a 5-10% dip is ordinary noise.

The project's own work shows the same thing at the bots' time scale. Most runners the bots bought fell 20c or more under the buy price before their top: **18 of 28 for v36, 15 of 22 for v36b, 48 of 56 for v37**, and all came back above the buy, a median 1.5-3 minutes after the low. Buying back each time the price reclaimed the buy price churned into far larger losses, because "for each runner there are many look-alikes that dip and do not come back." Of v31's 46 trades from 09-30 to 10-05, only 2 won, yet **24 of the 46 went 3% or more above the entry within an hour of the sale**: "the scanner finds movers; the bot buys at a short-term top and sells on the dip" [Own log] ([journal](../memory/journal.md)). A replay of v36b's 10c stop against a 3% stop found the wider stop "gives back on the failures what it saves on the one that comes back" [Own log] ([journal](../memory/journal.md)). That is the honest shape of the problem. Widening stops alone does not fix it. The bot has to tell the runner from the look-alike, either at the buy or with a slower confirmation, and no public study shows how.

### Real news drifts; promotions, pumps and launch releases fade

The academic evidence on what predicts continuation is about information. Moves backed by analyst revisions show momentum while unconfirmed shocks reverse [Peer-reviewed] ([Govindaraj et al., via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2013/03/follow-the-trend-and-the-sell-side/)). The "extreme winner" reversal flips to continuation when the big day came from an earnings report [Peer-reviewed] ([Baars & Mohrschladt](https://cris-portal.uni-muenster.de/portal/en/publication/80040415)). Biotech approvals often "sell the news": Verastem fell as much as 21% after its first approval, having risen 191% that year [Anecdote] ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2018/09/25/why-verastem-inc-stock-is-crashing-today.aspx)).

Promotion-driven spikes reverse within days. Small caps with a sudden burst of social-media ticker posts rose a reported 6.5% on the event day and fell about 2.5% over the next five, with a stronger reversal when promoters posted [Working paper; figures from a secondary summary] ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=30149)). FINRA describes the typical "ramp-and-dump" target: **an IPO raising under $25 million, with fewer than 20 million shares, valued under $100 million, usually with operations abroad**, promoted through chat groups [Regulator] ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/ramp-and-dump-schemes)). From late September 2025 the SEC suspended a string of foreign small caps, including QMMM after a rise of nearly 1,000% in under three weeks, and exchanges then kept trading halted for months [Regulator, via law firms and press] ([Securities Lawyer 101](https://www.securitieslawyer101.com/2025/sec-trading-suspensions-of-qmmm-sdm-new-sec-cross-border-task-force/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bloomberg-government-news/sec-foreign-firm-suspension-blitz-spurs-monthslong-trading-halts)). Nasdaq proposed higher float minimums in September 2025, citing "emerging patterns associated with potential pump-and-dump schemes"; approval was not confirmed [Regulator] ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/nasdaq-proposes-changes-its-listing-standards-2025-09-03)). Holding such a name risks a weeks-long halt with no exit.

### Float and relative volume are folklore without published tests

Theory explains why small floats run: with shorting constrained and speculators overconfident, **a smaller float produces a bigger bubble**, more turnover and more volatility, and prices drop when the float expands [Peer-reviewed] ([Hong, Scheinkman & Xiong 2006](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v61y2006i3p1073-1117.html)). Beyond that, the numbers are course material. Steven Dux's material reports post-open pushes of roughly 30-35% on 1-2 million share floats against 20-25% on 5-10 million [Vendor, course] ([Tradezella](https://www.tradezella.com/strategies/small-cap-short-strategy)). Ross Cameron's "five pillars" (relative volume of 5x or more, up 10%+, news, $1-$20, float under 10 million) circulate as a third-party script with no published win rates [Practitioner] ([TradingView](https://kr.tradingview.com/script/mbqMf3pF-Ross-Cameron-5-Pillars-Filter)). The only public gradient of edge against relative volume is the opening-range paper's buckets. For ordinary stocks, unusually high volume predicts a rise over the next month [Peer-reviewed] ([Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/9901.pdf)), but high turnover in stocks with little institutional ownership, which describes low-float runners, predicts underperformance [Peer-reviewed] ([Nagel 2005](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/357572)). No published large-sample test of float size, float rotation or relative-volume thresholds for same-day continuation was found. The project's own recorded days are the only place to measure them.

### Dilution and borrow are known, checkable risks

Small issuers can sell stock into a spike quickly. At-the-market programs became available to smaller firms in 2008, and by 2016 their number reached 63% of traditional follow-on offerings [Peer-reviewed] ([Billett, Floros & Garfinkel](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v54y2019i03p1263-1283_00.html)). DilutionTracker, from over 1,300 offerings, reports that offerings are often preceded by a spike of more than 50% and push the price down **20-30% on announcement**, worse with more warrants [Vendor] ([DilutionTracker](https://knowledge.dilutiontracker.com/en/articles/5722415-offerings-investment-bank-tiers-and-why-it-matters-for-small-cap-stocks)). Registered direct offerings without warrants were followed by market-adjusted returns of -4.44% in year one and **-25.59% over three years**, in a sample that excluded the smallest issuers, so real outcomes are likely worse [Working paper] ([Kennesaw State](https://www.kennesaw.edu/coles/research/docs/fall-2024/fall-24-04.pdf)). Reverse-split firms show negative three-year abnormal returns and are "very difficult to sell short" [Peer-reviewed] ([Kim, Klein & Rosenfeld](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finmgt/v37y2008i2p173-192.html)), though one conference paper finds the opposite for small firms ([IAES](https://iaes.confex.com/iaes/72am/webprogram/Paper6539.html)). A live shelf registration or at-the-market program is public, checkable information. Under the owner's rule, a loss from something known and predictable is a defect, so this check belongs in any position held past the first push or overnight. No public data exist on borrow cost or locate availability for day-one runners.

## One-minute bars cannot judge a five-second rule

### Bars hide the order of prices, the spread and the size

A one-minute bar shows four prices and hides the order they came in. The same bar can mean opposite trades: a path from 100 to 111 to 94 exits at the target, while 100 to 94 to 111 exits at the stop, and only tick data can tell them apart ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/GuilhermeFortuna/q_backend/issues/39)). Most engines simply assume the stop came first ([GitHub PR](https://github.com/tonycccccc/Quant/pull/1)). Bars also hide the spread, the size at the quote, the queue, halts and cancelled prints; cancelled trades stay in tick files with correction flags and are dropped from bars [Vendor] ([Massive](https://massive.com/knowledge-base/article/which-timestamps-are-returned-for-massives-stock-trades-and-nbbo-quotes)). The project's replay runs on one-minute bars with invented fills and spreads. Its own journal records that a 7-day replay looked the same with or without a 5-second chase rule, because "1-minute bars cannot show a 5-second chase," and that almost all of the replay's big-move gain came from a single trade [Own log] ([journal](../memory/journal.md)). A cheap first check costs nothing: count how many replayed trades hit both the stop and the target inside one bar, rerun the replay stop-first and target-first, and treat any rule whose total swings between the two as unjudgeable on bars.

An honest replay for this bot feeds every consolidated trade and best bid and ask in time order through the same code that runs live. It buys at the ask and sells at the bid, after a delay measured from the bot's own logs (decision to fill is about 1.3 seconds live [Own log]). It caps each fill at the size shown at the quote, fills a resting limit only when the price trades through it, fills nothing during a halt, applies trade cancellations, and treats premarket spreads as wide. It is then calibrated against the bots' saved live fills. Event-driven tools such as NautilusTrader model quote-by-quote fills, latency and the size a fill uses up, though even they cannot see how the bot's own order would have moved the market ([NautilusTrader docs](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/concepts/backtesting/)), and one recent release filled twice the traded size under certain settings ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/issues/5262)). Moving the rules into any outside framework creates a second copy that can drift from the live code. Upgrading the existing replay to feed the same live code a trades-plus-quotes stream keeps one copy of the strategy.

### Every rule tried uses up evidence

Each extra threshold tested on the same days raises the odds that the best one won by luck. With five years of daily data, trying **45 or more independent variations** makes a Sharpe ratio of 1.0 or better likely by chance alone; with two years, no more than about 7 should be tried [Working paper] ([Bailey et al.](https://sdm.lbl.gov/oapapers/ssrn-id2507040-bailey.pdf)). Because finance has been mined so heavily, a new finding should clear a t-statistic of 3, not the usual 2 [Peer-reviewed] ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)). Tools exist to correct for this: the probability of backtest overfitting, published in the Journal of Computational Finance ([SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)), and the deflated Sharpe ratio, published in the Journal of Portfolio Management ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)) [Peer-reviewed]. Both need an honest count of every variant tried, including the rejected ones, which means keeping a dated trial log. Trades on the same day share one market mood, so they are not independent; count days as well as trades.

### A 40% win rate at 2-to-1 needs 200 to 500 trades to prove

The table below is the researchers' own arithmetic, not from a source. It assumes every trade ends at exactly +2R or -1R, independently, with cost taken off both outcomes. The break-even win rate at 2-to-1 is 33.3%.

| Win rate | Cost per trade | Edge per trade | Trades for ordinary proof (t = 2) | Trades if many variants were tried (t = 3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35% | none | +0.05R | about 3,150 | about 7,400 |
| 35% | 0.1R | -0.05R | no edge | no edge |
| 40% | none | +0.20R | about 210 | about 490 |
| 40% | 0.1R | +0.10R | about 830 | about 1,940 |
| 45% | none | +0.35R | about 70 | about 165 |
| 45% | 0.1R | +0.25R | about 140 | about 320 |

With 100 trades and a measured 40% win rate, the true rate could reasonably be anywhere from about **30% to 50%**, which spans "losing after costs" to "strong edge" [Arithmetic]. At an assumed four qualifying trades a day, 210 trades is about 50 trading days and 490 is about 120. Real small-cap trades have fat tails (a few big runners, a few gaps through stops), which raises these counts further, consistent with the finding that negative skew and fat tails lengthen the record needed ([PortfolioOptimizer on minimum track record length](https://portfoliooptimizer.io/blog/the-probabilistic-sharpe-ratio-bias-adjustment-confidence-intervals-hypothesis-testing-and-minimum-track-record-length/)). Comparing two versions of a rule is cheaper if both run on the same signals, which a tick replay allows. A month of results, on three different strategies, cannot yet separate an edge from noise in either direction.

## A small bot can cut costs and choose better, not outrun the market

The evidence points to a handful of changes a retail-scale bot can make. None is proven for this universe; each is a market reason plus a known cost, and each must be judged on both sides, by the losers it removes and the good moves it blocks.

| Change | Why (evidence) | What it may cost |
|---|---|---|
| Trade only the day's top one or two names by relative volume or money traded | Edge rises steeply with relative volume (opening-range paper, Working paper). Over three days, v36's first trade each day made +$268 while all its trades lost $1,718 (Own log, 3 days, one big winner) | Misses later movers; small sample |
| Require a checkable catalyst; treat launch/partnership releases, social-media-only moves and tiny foreign IPOs as fade-prone | News-backed moves drift, no-news moves reverse (Peer-reviewed); promotion profile (Regulator); launch overshoot (Working paper) | Checking news in seconds is hard; some promoted names still run |
| Buy at the ask, or cap any pay-up as a share of the spread, instead of ask plus 20c | 12 trades with the bid already under the stop cost $1,519 (Own log). A replay of v37 buying at the ask cut 114 trades' loss from $1,349 to $525-$848, with 89-99% of shares still bought (Own log, replay, generous to first-in-line) | More missed fills on real breakouts |
| Do not buy when the spread is as wide as the stop | Mechanics: such a stop is hit at the moment of the fill | Blocks some runners whose premarket spread is wide |
| Exit premarket with a limit capped below the bid, sized to what the bid shows | Limit-only rules; no bands; thin books (Broker docs, Regulator) | Caps the slippage, does not remove it |
| Do not hold a runner into the afternoon or overnight without a fresh reason; check for a shelf or at-the-market program first | About 2/3 close below the open (Vendor); offerings knock 20-30% (Vendor); suspensions (Regulator) | Misses the runners that go all day |
| Judge rules on a tick-and-quote replay, then on 200-500 live trades, with a trial log | Bars cannot judge seconds; overfitting math (Peer-reviewed); trade counts (Arithmetic) | Weeks to months before any verdict |

Some goals are likely out of reach. **Winning a speed race into 5-second bursts** is the clearest. The bots' decision-to-fill time is about 1.3 seconds, and paper sales took 0.9-3.9 seconds to fill [Own log]. In a five-second burst, that delay is a quarter of the burst's whole life, so a retail bot that buys bursts will mostly buy their tops. The Brazilian study found that high-frequency trading presence hurt individual traders ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fgv:eesptd:525)). **Price improvement in the premarket** is not documented anywhere. **The short side**, for most of these names, is closed on Alpaca's easy-to-borrow rule, and where locates exist their fee is roughly the size of the edge the anomaly studies find. **Reliable published edge sizes** do not exist for this universe; every win rate in circulation is vendor, course or practitioner material. And **a fast verdict** is not available: at realistic costs, separating a real edge from luck takes hundreds of trades, not weeks.

Two hard questions stay open, and only the bots' own data can answer them. Does entering after the first pullback, rather than into the burst, improve results net of the runners it misses? Passive-order evidence warns that waiting changes which trades fill, toward the losers. And can the runner be told from its look-alikes at the moment of the buy? The journal already states the problem plainly; no public study has solved it.

## Conclusion

The public evidence reframes what is wrong. The bots' scanner is not the weak part: news-driven, high-attention stocks do move, and the bots' own records show about half the names they sold went 3% or more above the entry within the hour. The weak part is that "momentum" entries on these stocks sit on the documented wrong side of their intraday behaviour. The open and the burst are rich, a deep dip is the norm, half-hour moves tend to reverse, and pauses are often liquidity gaps. A tight stop on such a stock buys the noise peak and sells the noise trough, paying a premarket spread three or more times wider than the daytime spread on both ends. Whatever edge exists lies in two places the literature points to but has not tested for this universe: choosing the one or two names with real news and extreme participation, and holding them through ordinary noise at a size the account can afford.

The honest starting assumption is that the bots have no edge yet, net of costs, and that real-money fills will be worse than paper. That is not a verdict against the owner's method. The Taiwan data show a small minority does win at this kind of trading, and what set them apart was what they traded, not how aggressively they ordered. But a profitable version, if one exists, will show up only as fewer trades, measured costs, quote-level replays, and a few hundred live trades judged against every variant tried. Short selling and speed-chasing are the two routes the evidence most clearly rules out for a bot of this size.
